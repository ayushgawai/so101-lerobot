## 1. Camera rate and teleop/recording practice

- Measured both USB cameras (gripper idx 0, top idx 1, 640x480 MJPG): **30 Hz** each.
- Teleop was running at its 60 Hz default, so each camera frame was reused twice. Run teleop and recording at **30 Hz**, the cameras' native rate (`--fps=30`, `--dataset.fps=30`).
- Practices adopted for data collection:
  - one rate everywhere (loop, dataset, cameras)
  - identical camera names, indices, resolution and MJPG between recording and eval
  - fixed camera mounts
  - check that the camera indices haven't swapped after a replug
  - only one live display at a time, if possible
- Rerun crashed with `memory limit 6.3 GiB` and gRPC `transport error`, because raw frames were streamed at 60 Hz. Use `--display_compressed_images=true`.
- Missing Rerun camera panels were a stale saved layout (`front`/`handeye` panels from an old session). In 0.6.1 the layout is generated automatically.

## 2. Error handling and recovery messages

`src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/so_follower.py`, `lerobot_teleoperate.py`, `lerobot_record.py`, `record_pickplace.py`
- If the follower fails to connect, it now releases the port and cameras and prints **stage-specific next steps**:
  - port didn't open → check the USB cable and COM port (`lerobot-find-port`)
  - camera failed → check the camera indices
  - motor fault → power-cycle; motor ID 5 = wrist_roll, 6 = gripper
- The leader and follower now disconnect **independently**, so one failure (for example a faulted motor) can't leave the other connected or skip the Hub push.
- Finding: a motor with a latched fault answers pings with an error flag, so the startup check reports it as **"missing motor ID"**. This was the original "ID 6 disconnects until power-cycle" symptom.

## 3. Gripper overload (motor 6)

Investigation, all measured on the arm:

| Observation | Result |
|---|---|
| Gripper registers | torque limit 50%; overload threshold 20–25%; protection time 2 s |
| Torque limit lowered to 18% | gripper still tripped Overload, and became slow and lagged the leader → **reverted** |
| Load vs. how far the goal is pushed past the cube | 50 ticks → 17%, 100 → 32%, ≥200 → 50% (saturated) |

**Cause:** with the leader fully closed, the follower is commanded hundreds of ticks past the cube. It then pushes at its 50% limit without moving, and after about 2 s it latches the Overload fault.

**Fix (stall relief, `SOFollower.send_action`):** if the gripper is blocked while closing for **0.5 s**, the command is capped at **50 ticks past where it stopped** (about 17% load). The cap releases as soon as you open the leader or the gripper moves again. Normal motion keeps full speed and torque.
- Constants: `GRIPPER_HOLD_SQUEEZE_TICKS = 50`, `GRIPPER_STALL_S = 0.5`. If the cube slips, raise the squeeze to 75 (about 25%).
- Still needs a long hold test on the real arm.

## 4. Record loop rate

- Warnings showed the loop at 6.7–25.8 Hz instead of 30. Per-stage timing:

  - Rerun logging inline: 6 ms median, spiking to 28 ms or more, and the loop stalled whenever the viewer lagged
- **Fix:** Rerun logging now runs on a background thread that keeps only the newest frame (`_BackgroundVisualizationLogger`). Measured: median loop 3.2 ms, worst case 18 ms, 0 loops over the 33 ms budget.

## 5. "Safe exit" torque warning

- `Failed to disable torque on motor 'gripper' … Overload error!` turned out to be a **false alarm**. A read-only probe showed torque was off on all six motors.
- Cause: Feetech motors put their fault flags in every reply, so a write they actually carried out looks failed. (The "after 6 tries" in the message is also misleading: no retries happened.)
- **Fix (`motors_bus.disconnect`):** on an error, read `Torque_Enable` back. If it's 0, the message now says *"latched hardware fault … torque is off, arm is safe; power-cycle before the next run."* A real failure still warns.

## 9. New free-placement recording flow (`run_record.ps1`)

`scripts/run_record.ps1` now wraps `scripts/record_episodes.py`. It works like `record_pickplace`, but has no position IDs.
- Per episode:
  1. The prompt asks you to place the cube and put the leader at the start pose; press **Enter** (or `q` to quit).
  2. The follower **slides to the leader's pose** over 1 s, unrecorded, so episodes don't start with a jump.
  3. Record with **no time limit**: **→** saves and prompts again, **←** discards and redoes, **Esc** stops.

  There's no reset phase.
- For ACT and SmolVLA, data stays consistent:
  - the same layout as the other datasets
  - **one task string per dataset**: appending with a different task is refused
  - the same safety checks as `record_pickplace`
- Default dataset: `aakashv100/so101-pick-place-random` (`hf_data/so101-pick-place-random`). Keep it separate from the position dataset, because their task strings differ.

## 10. Free-placement dataset: recording and analysis

Recorded tonight, 20:55–21:49 PDT, with `run_record.ps1` on 0.6.1, which also served as the first on-arm test of the new flow.
- 50 episodes, 64,797 frames at 30 fps (36 min of footage), task *"Pick up the cube and place it in the box"*.
- Cameras: top_cam + gripper_cam, 640×480 H.264. The scene is a white cube on a light wood table, with a translucent box at a fixed spot.

**Integrity: clean.** No NaN values, no timestamp gaps, and frame indices run without breaks in every episode. Every episode has exactly one grasp and one release, with no regrasps, so there are no failed demos to filter.

**Cube positions vary much more than in v2.** The cube was located in each episode's first top_cam frame by background subtraction. The detected position agrees with the base rotation (shoulder_pan) at grasp, correlation −0.82, so the detections are reliable.

| Spread at grasp (std) | v2 (pick-cube) | random |
|---|---|---|
| Base rotation (shoulder_pan) | 0.8° | **20.3°** (−91° to +13°) |
| Wrist roll (cube orientation) | 3.2° | 17.5° |
| Reach (shoulder_lift) | 8.8 | 20.8 |

- **Caveat: the positions are clustered.** 34 of 50 episodes fall into six spots, each within about 1.7 cm (cluster sizes 9 / 7 / 6 / 5 / 4 / 3). Only about 10 episodes cover the edges (episodes 4, 5, 6, 19, 20, 32, 36, 40, 41, 44). Nothing is placed left of the main cluster or between the box and the arm.
- Expect the policy to do well at the cluster spots and worse at the edges. Keep the eval positions inside the recorded area, and score cluster spots and edge spots separately.
- The box location is fixed (base rotation 38.7 ± 1.3° at release). Keep it there during eval.
- Visual variation: in about episodes 0–11 the gripper-cam cable lies across the table; later it's routed away. Episodes 14–15 have a slightly green white balance. Make the eval scene look like the later episodes.

**Timing.** Episodes are slow: about 43 s on average (32–62 s), roughly 14 s to reach the cube and 15 s to carry it. 12.4% of frames are idle at the start or end, with a median of 112 frames (3.7 s) before the reach starts. That's the pattern behind SmolVLA's start-up stalls (`SmolVLA_training_report.md`), so the idle frames were trimmed (§11).

**Gripper offset (new; investigate).**
- With the gripper closed on nothing, the follower reads **38** while the leader commands **0–5**; in v2 it closed to 1.6. Holding the cube it reads 59.
- So the follower is always commanded about 38 units past where it can physically go, and a policy will learn to do the same. Stall relief (§3) limits the strain, but this is the overload pattern.
- Check what blocks the fingers (cam mount, pad) or whether the closed end of the calibration moved. Fixing it changes what "closed" means, so don't append new episodes to this dataset afterwards.

**Don't mix this dataset with v2.** They differ in:
- the task string (box vs. bowl)
- the wrist_roll calibration (mean +10 here vs. −100 in v2)
- the place location (base rotation +39° vs. −26°)
- the gripper offset above

## 11. Trimming idle frames (`scripts/trim_idle_frames.py`)

- **Ported to 0.6.1.** The first full run failed with `ValueError: ... shape '(3, 480, 640)' does not have the expected shape '(480, 640, 3)'`. In 0.6.1, `add_frame` accepts only height × width × channel images, matching what the cameras record, but the source dataset returns them channel-first. The script now reorders each camera image before writing it.
  - Verified on episode 35 into a scratch folder first: 905 frames, joint states identical to the source, images within 0.7% (re-encoding loss).
- Dry run: 64,797 → **58,251** frames kept (89.9%); cut 4,821 lead-in + 1,725 trailing frames, keeping 15 idle frames at each end.
- Full run: 22:00–22:59, about 1.2 min per episode. Output is in `%USERPROFILE%\.cache\huggingface\lerobot\aakashv100\so101-pick-place-random-trimmed`.
- The torchcodec DLL tracebacks at startup are harmless. LeRobot falls back to pyav for decoding.
- Re-encoded videos come out as **AV1** (the LeRobot default for new datasets) instead of H.264, about 2× larger. Training reads them fine.
- **Handover at the cut points is smooth.**
  - Each trimmed episode starts at the resting pose. The arm moves at about 0.5 units/frame over the first 5 frames, versus 1.45 typical while moving, and reaches about 2.9 by frame 15.
  - Every episode ends fully still (speed 0.00 over the last 5 frames).
- **Exception: episode 6.** Recording started while the follower was still finishing the 1 s slide to the leader's pose: for 7 frames (0.23 s) the leader is still while the follower settles about 4–5 units. That drift crossed the motion threshold at frame 0, so nothing was cut, and the real reach only starts at frame 132 (4.4 s). That's about 117 idle frames (0.2% of the data); left as is. If it happens again, `record_episodes.py` should wait for the follower to stop moving before recording.

## 13. Train/validation split for SmolVLA

- There's no split in the dataset itself (`splits: train 0:50`).
- `train_smolvla.ps1 -ValEpisodes` holds whole episodes out of training and scores `val/loss` on every frame every `-ValFreq` steps.
- There's no offline test set; the on-arm eval is the test.
- Recommended holdout: **`(47,45,37,42,39)`**, one episode from each of the five largest clusters. Every held-out position still has neighbours in training, and no edge positions are lost. (`45..49` would also avoid the edges, but 46, 48 and 49 are scattered rather than cluster repeats.)
- The val loss measures generalization to new demos at positions already in training, not to new positions; the normalization statistics also cover all 50 episodes. SmolVLA's flow-matching loss is noisy, so use it to catch overfitting, not to predict success on the arm.
- Cost: about 5,500 frames at roughly 19 frames/s, so about 5 min per pass and about 50 min extra over 20k steps.
